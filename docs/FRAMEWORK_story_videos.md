## 2. Rules as the story goes on (the question: does the author lay down rules, and are there unsaid ones?)

Yes, on four levels, and the videos use all four:

1. **Stated rules**: said aloud, numbered or named, and repeated as a refrain every time they are used (Train: "Rule one, never stop. Rule two, answer the beep"; Bunker: "50 lbs moves that door" returns eight times).
2. **Discovered rules**: not given by anyone; the person learns them from something that just happened (Train: "The dead go for noise", "They push, they can't pull" at the window handle and the cooler knob).
3. **Policies nobody reads out**: a hierarchy or procedure the story shows by what happens (Bunker: Crane's two lists, staff sleep nearest the big door, "Staff can listen" at every vote; Train: Ruth's board shows only your wheels, so she chooses the route). The person feels them first and names them later.
4. **A rule that is a lie**: an authority's rule or number holds the secret (School: "stay put"; Train: "never stop"; Bunker: "one year"). The twist is learning the rule was there to serve the authority.

One rule is usually broken on purpose near the end, and the ending changes what the first rule meant. The simulator today has rules for the AI (hard, world, tone, style, craft) and, since the Story Architect, rules the person discovers. It had no policy rules, no repeat marks on rules, and no rule broken on purpose; the plan adds them.

## 3. What this means for the build

Everything above is a property of the plan, not of the prose. A model that is handed the plan can only write what the plan promises, so the plan must contain: the ladder with its signposts and solving facts, the authority-lie secret with the method that finds it, the on-screen number and its steps, the objects that are planted and paid off, the act skeleton (midpoint, low point), rules of four kinds with their refrain beats, and an ending that turns the opening around.
